Interfacing quantum systems with light is crucial for advancing quantum technology,
which stands as a defining challenge of our era. Molecular
impurities embedded in solid hosts hold immense potential, yet they currently face several hurdles, includ-
ing: (i) a limited comprehension of their behavior, (ii) the absence of a clear exploration strategy to unlock
their vast potential, and (iii) their static and nondeterministic implementation within solids, a characteristic
shared with many alternative approaches. We develop: (i) a predictive theory
for molecular impurities in host materials based on first principles. (ii) A
systematic machine-learning approach to identify new molecular
guest-host matches. (iii) Smart molecule-host systems which
allow dynamic (un)loading of the molecular impurity – a miniature version of cold atoms in optical traps.
